# KPR Code of Conduct

KPR is an open-source Excel/VBA library for financial analytics and instrument
pricing. Its community should support rigorous technical discussion while
remaining respectful, constructive, and welcoming.

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in KPR a harassment-free experience for
everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity
and expression, level of experience, nationality, personal appearance, race,
religion, sexual identity and orientation, socioeconomic status, or other
personal characteristics.

We commit to acting and interacting in ways that contribute to an open,
welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Expected behavior

Participants are expected to:

- be respectful and assume good faith;
- focus criticism on code, models, assumptions, documentation, evidence, and
  observable behavior rather than on the people involved;
- distinguish facts, conventions, assumptions, hypotheses, and opinions;
- state uncertainty and correct mistakes when better evidence becomes
  available;
- provide reproducible examples and enough environment information to support
  investigation;
- welcome questions from people with different levels of VBA, Excel, finance,
  and numerical-methods experience;
- give and receive technical feedback constructively;
- respect privacy, confidentiality, licensing, and intellectual-property
  boundaries; and
- accept that maintainers may adopt, revise, defer, or decline a contribution
  to preserve project scope, quality, and compatibility.

## Unacceptable behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- harassment, intimidation, discrimination, or personal attacks;
- trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and inflammatory language;
- unwelcome sexual attention or advances;
- publishing private or confidential information without permission;
- deliberately misrepresenting results, sources, authorship, test evidence, or
  financial-model behavior;
- pressuring others to disclose employer, client, student, or proprietary
  information;
- repeated disruption of a technical discussion after a maintainer has asked
  participants to stop; and
- other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Technical discussion standards

Disagreement is useful when it can be investigated. When reporting or debating
behavior, provide the relevant information where practical:

- the KPR revision or release;
- Excel and Windows versions and Office bitness;
- the smallest reproducible input;
- the financial conventions used, such as day count, business-day adjustment,
  calendar, compounding, settlement, price or rate quotation, and sign
  convention;
- expected and observed outputs, including tolerances;
- an independent reference or derivation when numerical accuracy is at issue;
  and
- evidence showing whether the problem is deterministic, data-dependent, or
  environment-dependent.

Sample portfolios, workbooks, screenshots, and logs must not contain client,
employer, student, personal, or otherwise confidential data. Replace sensitive
data with a minimal synthetic example.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all KPR project spaces, including issues, pull
requests, discussions, reviews, documentation, release activity, and other
public spaces where someone is representing the project or its community.

## Enforcement

The maintainer is responsible for clarifying and enforcing this Code of Conduct
and may remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and other
contributions that are inconsistent with it.

Possible responses include a private clarification, a warning, moderation of
project content, temporary restriction, permanent exclusion from project
spaces, or escalation to GitHub. Enforcement will be proportionate to the
nature, context, and persistence of the behavior.

Ordinary conduct concerns may be raised in the relevant issue or pull request.
Do not publish sensitive details. If a public report would expose personal or
confidential information, contact the maintainer through an established private
channel or use GitHub's reporting facilities.

Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern or participates in its review
is itself a violation.
